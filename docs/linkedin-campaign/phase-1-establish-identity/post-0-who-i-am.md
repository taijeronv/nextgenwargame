## 1. Post Draft

I did not discover AI through CADE.

CADE is where I learned what it takes to use AI professionally.

Before CADE, I was already exploring what AI could do. I was testing tools, workflows, and ways to make hard knowledge work move faster.

But CADE changed the standard.

CADE is a military tabletop exercise for training battalion and brigade staffs. I designed and developed it to help staffs practice judgment under pressure in a realistic but manageable training environment.

The important part is not that I used AI to help build it.

The important part is how the work had to begin.

It did not begin with prompting.

It began with planning.

Before I generated exercise content, I had to define the training problem. I had to understand the audience, the constraints, the available time, the delivery method, and the outcome the training actually needed to produce.

That discovery work shaped everything that followed.

It told me what AI should produce.

It told me what AI should not decide.

It told me where human judgment had to stay in control.

That is where my view of AI changed.

AI is powerful, but it does not remove the need for professional thinking. It makes that thinking more important.

CADE taught me that good AI work is not just about building faster.

It is about knowing what should be built, why it should be built, and where the human has to remain responsible.

That is the kind of AI work I want to keep doing.
